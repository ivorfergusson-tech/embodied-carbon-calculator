1. Project assumptions

Why are these project-level? (click to expand)

Transport and construction-site energy aren't really properties of a material, they depend on the job. So, like the yellow input cells in the workbook, they're set once here and every row below uses them. A5 is a flat % uplift because detailed site-energy data isn't normally available this early. RICS whole life carbon guidance allows a default assumption when project-specific data is missing.

2. Materials take-off

How each row is calculated (click to expand)

Mass incl. waste = quantity × density (if taken off in m³) or quantity (if already in kg), × (1 + waste allowance). Real orders always include offcuts and over-ordering, so carbon is worked out on what's bought, not just what ends up in the structure.

A1–A3 = mass × ICE carbon factor. A4 = tonnes × distance × transport factor. A5 = (A1–A3 + A4) × site allowance %. Change any quantity or material and the total updates live.

4. Carbon factors used

Where do these numbers come from? (click to expand)

Material factors are based on the ICE Database (Inventory of Carbon and Energy), first built at the University of Bath and now maintained by Circular Ecology. It's the standard free UK reference for embodied carbon. They're representative cradle-to-gate (A1–A3) figures, not product-specific EPDs. A real project would use the supplier's EPD and actual delivery details.

5. Scope and limitations

  • Embodied, not operational. This covers carbon from making, transporting and building, not from heating and lighting the building once it's in use.
  • Stages A1–A5 only (BS EN 15978). It leaves out B (maintenance and replacement) and C (demolition and disposal), which a full whole-life carbon assessment would include.
  • One transport distance and vehicle type for every material. A real project would use each supplier's actual delivery distance.
  • Timber excludes biogenic carbon. Growing trees absorb CO₂. Some methods count that as a negative figure, but it's excluded here, so timber is if anything shown less favourably than it really is.
  • Factors are illustrative. Check the live ICE Database (v4.1) or product EPDs before relying on any figure.
